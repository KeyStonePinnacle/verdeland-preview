New Construction Warranties in New York: What Nassau County Buyers Should Know

Published October 21, 2026

New York provides a 1-, 2- and 6-year warranty framework for qualifying new homes. Each period covers a different category, and six-year coverage is narrowly defined. Your purchase agreement may instead contain a legally compliant limited warranty. Read the actual documents before signing, not just a listing's warranty summary.

The New York Attorney General's buyer guidance explains the basic framework. The controlling details are in General Business Law Article 36-B.

Which homes does the law cover?

The definition includes single-family houses and for-sale condominium or cooperative units in residential structures of five stories or fewer. It excludes lease-only dwellings, specified mobile homes, and homes the builder continuously occupied or leased for at least three years after documented completion.

A renovated existing home does not automatically qualify merely because its finishes are new. Have counsel assess unusual conversions, retained structures and the transaction itself.

The statutory start date is the earlier of qualifying transfer of title to the first owner or first residential occupancy. Under the implied warranty, successors generally receive the unexpired coverage, not a new clock. These definitions appear in GBL §777.

What does the 1-, 2- and 6-year framework mean?

  • One year: defects resulting from construction that was not performed skillfully.
  • Two years: defects resulting from unskillful installation of the defined plumbing, electrical, heating, cooling and ventilation systems.
  • Six years: statutory material defects.

The six-year category requires actual physical damage caused by failure of specified load-bearing portions, impairing their function enough to make the home unsafe, unsanitary or otherwise unlivable. It does not cover every crack or every roof problem for six years. “Skillful” construction is measured against applicable code standards or, where no relevant specific standard exists, locally accepted building practices. See §777 definitions and §777-a coverage.

Can a builder offer a different written warranty?

Yes, but a replacement limited warranty must comply with §777-b. Among other requirements, the buyer must receive its written terms before signing, and those terms must be incorporated into the agreement. Its coverage periods must equal or exceed the statutory periods; standards cannot fall below applicable code or local practice, or permit unsafe, uninhabitable conditions.

The document must identify coverage, exclusions, warrantors and claim procedures. It may exclude subsequent owners. Do not assume all warranties transfer or that a broad disclaimer alone removes the statutory protection. Have counsel review the proposed replacement against GBL §777-b.

Builder warranty, manufacturer warranty or service contract?

These terms describe different protections:

  • A builder warranty addresses covered construction defects and identifies the responsible warrantor.
  • A manufacturer's warranty concerns a particular product, subject to its terms. Keep appliance and equipment documents separately.
  • A paid home-service contract may cover certain repairs or replacements, with its own fees, exclusions and limits. It should not be treated as a substitute for understanding your new-home warranty.

The Federal Trade Commission's warranty guide explains these distinctions. Ask who handles a product failure versus an installation problem.

Does coverage change by state, county, town or city?

State law can differ substantially. For example, New Jersey's program describes 1-, 2- and 10-year periods. Do not import that ten-year figure into a New York purchase.

Within New York, Article 36-B supplies the statewide framework for homes within its scope and preempts inconsistent local laws. Local builder regulation and applicable construction standards still matter. Nassau County is not part of New York City, and “New York County” means Manhattan alone. NYC contains five boroughs and five corresponding counties, as explained by NYC311.

NYC also has its own construction code, while the state Uniform Code applies elsewhere, subject to authorized local standards. The Department of State's explanation helps distinguish code enforcement from warranty obligations.

What should you do if you find a possible defect?

Document the condition, preserve records and notify the responsible parties promptly in writing using the warranty's instructions. Keep proof of receipt and obtain professional advice for urgent or disputed conditions.

For a statutory implied-warranty claim, §777-a requires the builder to receive written notice before litigation and no later than 30 days after the applicable warranty period expires. The owner and occupant must allow a reasonable opportunity to inspect, test and repair. Litigation deadlines are separate and include special rules; ask counsel to calculate them rather than treating a repair request as stopping the clock.

Coverage also has limits, including specified visible defects that an examination should have revealed in the circumstances. Review §777-a with the warranty documents.

Frequently asked questions

Does “six-year warranty” mean everything is covered for six years?

No. Confirm the category of defect, applicable period, exclusions and claim procedure. Avoid relying on that shorthand alone.

Does a resale restart the warranty?

Do not assume it does. Ask for the original start date and written confirmation of any remaining transferable coverage.

Is this article VerdeLand's warranty?

No. This is general buyer education. Request the actual warranty and purchase agreement for the specific VerdeLand property you are considering.

What should I ask before signing?

Ask who is responsible, when coverage begins, what is excluded, whether coverage transfers, how to submit a claim and which documents govern. Have your attorney review the answers and agreement together.

This guide is general information, not legal advice or a determination that a particular defect is covered. Laws and contract terms should be checked for your transaction.

Limits and exclusions apply. The executed warranty and purchase documents govern, subject to applicable law.
